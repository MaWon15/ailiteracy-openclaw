# Week 5 Lecture Notes: AI Ethics and Safety

## Why AI Ethics Matters

### Societal Impact
- **Scale**: AI systems affect millions of people
- **Autonomy**: AI makes decisions independently
- **Opacity**: Complex systems are hard to understand
- **Irreversibility**: Some AI decisions cannot be undone

### Historical Context
- **Industrial Revolution**: Technology changed society dramatically
- **Nuclear Age**: Technology with destructive potential
- **Digital Age**: Data and algorithms shape our world
- **AI Age**: Intelligence itself becomes automated

## Core Ethical Principles

### Beneficence (Do Good)
- **Positive impact**: AI should improve human well-being
- **Harm prevention**: Avoid foreseeable negative consequences
- **Long-term thinking**: Consider future generations

### Non-Maleficence (Do No Harm)
- **Safety first**: Implement robust safety measures
- **Fail-safes**: Graceful degradation when systems fail
- **Accountability**: Clear responsibility for AI actions

### Autonomy (Respect Freedom)
- **Human agency**: Don't undermine human decision-making
- **Informed consent**: Users understand AI involvement
- **Privacy protection**: Respect personal data boundaries

### Justice (Fair Treatment)
- **Equal access**: Benefits distributed fairly
- **Bias mitigation**: Avoid discrimination
- **Transparency**: Explainable decision processes

## Technical Safety

### System Safety
- **Verification**: Prove systems meet specifications
- **Validation**: Test systems in realistic conditions
- **Redundancy**: Backup systems for critical functions
- **Monitoring**: Continuous performance tracking

### AI Safety Challenges
- **Unintended consequences**: Systems optimize for wrong objectives
- **Reward hacking**: Finding loopholes in reward functions
- **Distributional shift**: Performance degrades in new environments
- **Adversarial inputs**: Malicious inputs that fool systems

### Alignment Problem
- **Value alignment**: AI goals match human values
- **Specification gaming**: Systems exploit imprecise objectives
- **Scalable oversight**: Human supervision at scale

## Bias and Fairness

### Sources of Bias
- **Training data**: Reflects historical inequalities
- **Algorithm design**: Implicit assumptions about fairness
- **Feedback loops**: Systems reinforce existing biases
- **Measurement bias**: Evaluation metrics favor certain groups

### Fairness Metrics
- **Demographic parity**: Equal outcomes across protected groups
- **Equal opportunity**: Equal true positive rates
- **Predictive equality**: Equal false positive rates
- **Calibration**: Reliable probability estimates across groups

### Mitigation Approaches
- **Pre-processing**: Modify training data
- **In-processing**: Fairness constraints during training
- **Post-processing**: Adjust predictions
- **Monitoring**: Ongoing fairness assessment

## Privacy and Data Ethics

### Data Collection
- **Consent**: Clear, informed permission
- **Purpose limitation**: Use data only for stated purposes
- **Data minimization**: Collect only necessary information
- **Retention limits**: Delete data when no longer needed

### Data Protection
- **Anonymization**: Remove personally identifiable information
- **Encryption**: Protect data in transit and at rest
- **Access controls**: Limit who can see sensitive data
- **Audit trails**: Track data usage

### Surveillance Concerns
- **Mass surveillance**: AI enables unprecedented monitoring
- **Predictive policing**: Preemptive law enforcement
- **Social credit systems**: Automated reputation scoring
- **Filter bubbles**: Algorithmic personalization

## Transparency and Accountability

### Explainable AI
- **Interpretability**: Humans can understand decisions
- **Traceability**: Track decision-making process
- **Auditability**: Independent verification possible
- **User control**: Ability to override or appeal decisions

### Governance Frameworks
- **Regulatory compliance**: Meet legal requirements
- **Industry standards**: Follow best practices
- **Ethical review boards**: Independent oversight
- **Impact assessments**: Evaluate societal consequences

### Liability and Responsibility
- **Corporate responsibility**: Companies accountable for AI systems
- **Developer ethics**: Professional responsibility
- **User rights**: Protection from harmful AI systems
- **Societal oversight**: Democratic control of AI development

## Case Studies

### Autonomous Vehicles
- **Trolley problem**: Who to protect in unavoidable accidents?
- **Safety vs privacy**: Data collection for safety improvements
- **Insurance implications**: Liability for AI decisions
- **Equity concerns**: Access to autonomous vehicle benefits

### Social Media Algorithms
- **Content moderation**: Balancing free speech and harm prevention
- **Echo chambers**: Algorithmic reinforcement of beliefs
- **Mental health**: Impact on user well-being
- **Election interference**: Manipulation through personalized content

### Hiring Algorithms
- **Resume screening**: Automated candidate evaluation
- **Bias amplification**: Historical hiring patterns
- **Transparency**: Explain hiring decisions
- **Human oversight**: Role of human recruiters

## Future Challenges

### Advanced AI Systems
- **Superintelligence**: AI surpassing human capabilities
- **Value alignment**: Ensuring beneficial goals
- **Control problem**: Maintaining human control
- **Existential risk**: Potential for catastrophic outcomes

### Societal Transformation
- **Job displacement**: Automation of human labor
- **Economic inequality**: Unequal distribution of AI benefits
- **Geopolitical tensions**: AI in international conflicts
- **Cultural impacts**: Changes to human relationships

### Governance Solutions
- **International cooperation**: Global AI governance
- **Research priorities**: Focus on beneficial AI
- **Education**: AI literacy for all citizens
- **Inclusive development**: Broad participation in AI progress

- Technical solutions must be complemented by governance
- Ethical AI development requires interdisciplinary collaboration
- Society must actively shape AI's role in our future
