# Chapter 1: Introduction
**Source:** Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach, 4th Edition — Russell & Norvig

## What Is AI?

Four competing definitions organize the field along two dimensions: human vs. rational behavior, and thought vs. action.

| | Human-centered | Rationality-centered |
|---|---|---|
| **Thought** | "Systems that think like humans" | "Systems that think rationally" |
| **Action** | "Systems that act like humans" | "Systems that act rationally" |

- **Turing Test approach:** A machine passes if an interrogator cannot distinguish it from a human in a text conversation. Required capabilities: natural language processing, knowledge representation, automated reasoning, machine learning.
- **Cognitive modeling approach:** Build systems that replicate human thought processes (cognitive science).
- **Laws of thought approach:** Use formal logic to encode correct reasoning (logicist tradition).
- **Rational agent approach (adopted by the textbook):** An agent acts to achieve the best expected outcome. Does not require human-like behavior — only optimality.

## The Foundations of AI

- **Philosophy:** Logic, rationality, mind-body problem, knowledge representation.
- **Mathematics:** Formal logic, probability, decision theory, computability.
- **Economics:** Utility theory, game theory, rational decision-making under uncertainty.
- **Neuroscience:** Brain structure, neural computation, parallel processing.
- **Psychology:** Behaviorism, cognitive psychology, perception and action.
- **Computer engineering:** Fast hardware, large memory, efficient algorithms.
- **Control theory / Cybernetics:** Feedback systems and optimal control.
- **Linguistics:** Language structure, knowledge representation, NLP.

## History of AI

- **1943–1955:** McCulloch & Pitts neural model; Turing's seminal paper "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" (1950).
- **1956:** Dartmouth Conference — birth of AI as a field. McCarthy coins the term "Artificial Intelligence."
- **1952–1969:** Early enthusiasm. General Problem Solver (Newell & Simon), checkers-playing program (Samuel), LISP.
- **1966–1973:** First AI winter. Combinatorial explosion, perceptron limitations (Minsky & Papert 1969).
- **1969–1979:** Knowledge-based systems. DENDRAL, MYCIN expert systems.
- **1980–1988:** Expert system boom and commercialization.
- **1987–1993:** Second AI winter. Collapse of Lisp machine market.
- **1990s–2000s:** Rise of machine learning, probabilistic methods, data-driven approaches.
- **2010s–present:** Deep learning revolution. AlphaGo, GPT, large language models, autonomous vehicles.

## State of the Art

Notable AI achievements:
- **Game playing:** Deep Blue (chess, 1997), AlphaGo (Go, 2016), AlphaStar (StarCraft II).
- **Autonomous vehicles:** Self-driving cars navigate real traffic.
- **Medical diagnosis:** Outperforms radiologists on specific imaging tasks.
- **Natural language:** GPT-class models pass many language understanding benchmarks.
- **Theorem proving:** Automated provers verify complex mathematical theorems.
- **Robotics:** Boston Dynamics locomotion, surgical robots.

## Risks and Benefits

- **Benefits:** Science acceleration, economic productivity, accessibility tools, discovery.
- **Risks:** Autonomous weapons, surveillance, job displacement, value misalignment, existential risk.
- **AI safety:** Ensuring AI systems behave as intended and aligned with human values.

## Key Terms

- **AI:** The field of building agents that act rationally.
- **Rational agent:** An entity that acts to maximize expected utility given its percepts.
- **Turing Test:** A behavioral criterion for machine intelligence.
- **Cognitive science:** The interdisciplinary study of mind and intelligence.
